# The Vendor Journey: Georgian Company Selling to the Georgian Government

## Overview

This document maps the full journey a Georgian business must navigate to compete for and win a government contract through the Georgian Electronic Government Procurement (Ge-GP) system. Georgia's procurement transformation is one of the most dramatic anti-corruption success stories in modern governance: a system built in 10 months for less than USD 1 million that dropped public perception of procurement corruption from 97% to 3%.

The Ge-GP system, operated by the Competition and State Procurement Agency (CSPA, formerly SPA), is fully electronic with zero paper. Bidding documents are free (previously USD 100-150), participation costs a nominal GEL 50 fee, and any interested party worldwide can observe tenders in real time. The system won a UN Public Service Award in 2012, and Transparency International ranked Georgia best among 19 Eastern European and Central Asian countries for procurement transparency. In 2014, the World Bank approved Ge-GP for Bank-financed projects -- a rare endorsement of a national e-procurement system.

Georgia's procurement is governed by the Law on Public Procurement, with a major new law adopted 9 February 2023 aligning with EU directives under the EU-Georgia Association Agreement. Key provisions took effect 1 January 2025, with additional provisions phasing in through 2029.

Georgia's GDP was approximately USD 33.8 billion in 2024. Government consumption is roughly 12.7% of GDP. Annual public procurement through the e-system totals approximately GEL 2.7 billion (roughly USD 1 billion), though Transparency International estimates an additional GEL 1.2 billion bypasses the competitive system through simplified procurement and exemptions.

**Exchange rate used throughout:** 1 GEL = approximately 0.37 USD (February 2026).

## Phase 1: The Qualification Gauntlet

Everything a Georgian business must do before it can submit its first bid on Ge-GP.

| Step | Action | Estimated Cost (GEL) | Estimated Cost (USD) | Notes |
|------|--------|---------------------|---------------------|-------|
| 1 | Register legal entity with National Agency of Public Registry (NAPR) | 100 (standard) / 200 (expedited) / 400 (same-day) | $37 / $74 / $148 | One of the world's fastest business registrations: 1-2 days standard. Requires charter/articles of association, founders' resolution, ID documents. Done at Public Service Hall or online via NAPR website |
| 2 | Register with Revenue Service (tax registration) | Free | Free | Obtain taxpayer identification number (TIN); registration through the Tax Service Portal (rs.ge). Corporate income tax is 15% flat rate (Estonian model: tax only on distributed profits since 2017) |
| 3 | Register for VAT (if applicable) | Free | Free | Mandatory when taxable turnover exceeds GEL 100,000 in any continuous 12-month period. Standard VAT rate is 18%. Application must be filed within 2 business days of exceeding threshold. Voluntary registration also available |
| 4 | Open business bank account | Free-500 | Free-$185 | Bank of Georgia or TBC Bank most common. Multi-currency accounts (GEL, USD, EUR) available. Requires company registration extract, director ID, tax registration |
| 5 | Register on Ge-GP portal (procurement.gov.ge) | Free | Free | Electronic registration on the Unified Electronic System of State Procurement. Requires legal entity documentation, TIN, authorized representative details. Registration is immediate once documents verified |
| 6 | Obtain digital signature/electronic certificate | 50-100 | $19-$37 | Required for electronic bid submission. Issued by certified Georgian certificate authorities |
| 7 | Establish financial standing | 500-5,000 | $185-$1,850 | Audited financial statements for larger contracts; bank references. Standards vary by procurement size. No equivalent of US DCAA-compliant accounting required |
| 8 | Obtain required insurance (if contract-specific) | 1,000-10,000/yr | $370-$3,700/yr | Not universally required for registration but specified in individual tender documents. Public liability and professional indemnity typical for larger contracts |
| 9 | Prepare bid bond/bank guarantee capability | Varies (2-5% of contract value) | Varies | Banks charge 1-3% annual fee on guarantee amount. Must have banking relationship capable of issuing guarantees. Bid security typically 2-5% of estimated contract value |
| 10 | Build company profile and past performance record | Opportunity cost: months-years | -- | Same Catch-22 as elsewhere: need contracts to build record, need record to win contracts. Subcontracting and smaller simplified tenders are common entry paths |

**Estimated Qualification Cost: GEL 1,650 - 16,000 (USD 610 - 5,920)**

**Timeline: 2-5 days for basic registration; 1-3 months for full readiness including banking and guarantees**

### Key Observations

- Steps 1-5 can be completed in as little as 2 days -- Georgia was ranked 7th globally in World Bank's Ease of Doing Business (2020) and led the world (tied with New Zealand) for fewest procedures to start a business
- NAPR registration through Public Service Hall is famously efficient: one-stop service with multiple government registrations handled simultaneously
- The Ge-GP portal registration is free and immediate, with no equivalent to the multi-week US SAM.gov process
- There is no requirement for specialized government accounting systems (no DCAA equivalent)
- The major cost barrier is bank guarantee capability (Step 9), which requires sufficient banking history and collateral
- The 15% corporate income tax (deferred until distribution) is among the lowest in Europe
- Georgia's VAT threshold of GEL 100,000 means many small contractors operate VAT-free

## Phase 2: The Bidding Process

What it takes to respond to a single tender through the Ge-GP system.

### Procurement Methods and Thresholds

| Method | Value Threshold (GEL) | Value Threshold (USD) | Description |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Direct/Simplified Procurement | Below 5,000 | Below $1,850 | Single-source or limited competition; procuring entity discretion |
| Simplified Electronic Tender (SET) | 5,000-200,000 | $1,850-$74,000 | Electronic reverse auction format; streamlined process; replaced single-source for this range |
| Electronic Tender (ET) | Above 200,000 | Above $74,000 | Full competitive electronic tender; mandatory publication and competition period |
| Consolidated Tender | Determined by Government | Varies | Conducted by Central Purchasing Body for common goods/services across multiple agencies |
| International Tender Notification | Above 2,000,000 (goods/services) / 4,000,000 (works) | Above $740,000 / $1,480,000 | Mandatory international publication; open to foreign bidders |

### Bidding Steps

| Step | Action | Estimated Cost (GEL) | Estimated Cost (USD) | Notes |
|------|--------|---------------------|---------------------|-------|
| 1 | Monitor Ge-GP portal for opportunities | Free (time cost) | Free | All tenders published on procurement.gov.ge; searchable by category, agency, value. Open data portal since 2018 provides additional analytics |
| 2 | Download and review tender documentation | Free | Free | Previously cost USD 100-150; now entirely free. Documents include technical specifications, evaluation criteria, contract terms, submission requirements |
| 3 | Submit clarification requests (if needed) | Free | Free | Questions submitted electronically through portal; answers published to all participants transparently |
| 4 | Prepare technical proposal | 500-15,000 | $185-$5,550 | Address technical specifications and qualification requirements. Complexity varies: a simple goods supply may require minimal documentation; construction or IT services require detailed methodology |
| 5 | Prepare pricing/financial proposal | Included in Step 4 | -- | For electronic tenders, pricing is submitted through the reverse auction mechanism. For simplified tenders, pricing is a key component of the bid |
| 6 | Obtain bid security (bank guarantee) | 500-50,000 (bank fees on guarantee) | $185-$18,500 | Typically 2-5% of estimated contract value. Bank charges 1-3% annual fee on guarantee amount. Released upon contract signing or within 30 days of award to unsuccessful bidders |
| 7 | Pay tender participation fee | 50 (standard) / 5,000 (consolidated) | $19 / $1,850 | GEL 50 for electronic tenders and simplified electronic tenders; GEL 5,000 for consolidated tenders. One of the lowest participation fees globally |
| 8 | Submit bid electronically through Ge-GP | Free | Free | Fully electronic submission using digital signature. System timestamps all submissions. Late submissions physically impossible -- system closes automatically |
| 9 | Participate in electronic reverse auction (if applicable) | Free (time cost) | Free | For electronic tenders, prices are driven down through automated reverse auction. System applies algorithm considering low price, economic effectiveness, or price/quality ratio depending on criteria specified |
| 10 | Evaluation and award decision | Waiting (days-weeks) | -- | Evaluation timeline is compressed compared to Western systems. Award published on portal with full transparency |
| 11 | Contract signing | Free | Free | Electronic contract execution through the portal |
| 12 | Challenge/complaint (if needed) | 2,000-10,000 (est.) | $740-$3,700 | Complaints filed with Dispute Resolution Board (DRB) through the portal. DRB reviews within 10 working days. Anyone who detects a potential law violation can file a complaint, not just bidders |

**Estimated Cost Per Bid: GEL 1,050 - 75,000 (USD 390 - 27,750)**

**Typical small/medium bid cost: GEL 2,000-10,000 (USD 740-3,700)**

**Win rate: ~25-35% for experienced bidders (estimated; higher than Western systems due to smaller competitive pool)**

### Evaluation Methods

The 2023 Law on Public Procurement (effective January 2025) introduced several evaluation mechanisms:

1. **Lowest Price** -- Traditional price-only evaluation; dominant method historically; the reverse auction mechanism drives prices down automatically
2. **Economic Effectiveness** -- Considers factors beyond price, including quality and lifecycle costs
3. **Price/Quality Best Proportion** -- Weighted evaluation balancing technical merit against price; aligned with EU "Most Economically Advantageous Tender" (MEAT) concept
4. **Electronic Reverse Auction** -- Automated system applying whichever criterion the procuring entity specified (price, economic effectiveness, or price/quality)

### Key Observations

- The GEL 50 participation fee is extraordinarily low by global standards -- a deliberate policy choice to maximize competition
- Free bid documents eliminated a significant barrier that previously excluded smaller firms
- The reverse auction mechanism creates strong downward price pressure, particularly for commoditized goods
- The system's real-time transparency means any citizen can observe the procurement process as it happens
- Transparency International identified that approximately 45% of public contracting (GEL 1.2 billion) still bypasses competitive procurement through simplified procurement and exemptions -- a significant gap
- The 2023 law's introduction of price/quality evaluation methods signals a shift from pure lowest-price toward best-value considerations
- The Dispute Resolution Board's 10-day review timeline is remarkably fast compared to US GAO's 100-day protest timeline

**Total Vendor Investment to Win and Complete First Contract: approximately GEL 19,350 (USD 7,160)**

*Excludes direct contract performance costs (labor, materials, etc.) and assumes a single successful bid. If the win rate is 30%, expect 3 bids before winning, raising total sunk cost to approximately GEL 35,000 (USD 12,950).*

### Full Lifecycle Key Observations

- **Entry to first bid: 6-10 weeks** -- Among the fastest government contractor onboarding processes globally
- **Total sunk cost: GEL 19,350 (USD 7,160)** -- Dramatically lower than the US (USD 50,000-350,000+), Australia (AUD 8,000-175,000+), or the UK
- **The system deliberately minimizes barriers to entry** -- Free registration, free documents, GEL 50 participation fee, all-electronic process
- **Bank guarantees are the largest single cost** -- This is a common pattern across procurement systems but the amounts are modest relative to contract value
- **Reverse auction pressure on margins** -- While entry costs are low, the competitive auction mechanism can compress profit margins significantly, particularly for commodity goods
- **Speed advantage** -- A Georgian company can go from no legal entity to contract award in approximately 12 weeks, compared to 6-18 months in most Western procurement systems

## Connection to Dissertation Research

### LPTA vs. Best-Value: Georgia as an Evolving Case Study

Georgia's procurement system provides a fascinating case study of a system transitioning from strict lowest-price toward best-value evaluation:

**Historical Default: Lowest Price Dominance**
- The Ge-GP system was built around electronic reverse auction, which by design drives prices to the lowest possible level
- This mirrors LPTA (Lowest Price Technically Acceptable) logic: once technical qualification is met, price determines the winner
- The system was explicitly designed this way to combat corruption -- removing human judgment from evaluation removes opportunities for bribery

**The 2023 Reform: Moving Toward Best-Value**
- The new Law on Public Procurement (adopted 9 February 2023, main provisions effective 1 January 2025) introduces:
  - **Economic effectiveness** criteria
  - **Price/quality best proportion** evaluation (aligned with EU MEAT concept)
  - **Framework agreements** for up to 4 years
  - **Dynamic purchasing systems** for commonly available goods
- These changes are driven by EU-Georgia Association Agreement obligations, representing the same convergence toward best-value that EU member states have undergone

**What This Reveals for the Dissertation**
1. **Anti-corruption vs. value optimization trade-off**: Georgia chose lowest-price as an anti-corruption strategy. The dramatic corruption reduction (97% to 3%) validates this approach for environments with endemic corruption. But as the system matures, the limitations of price-only evaluation become apparent -- quality issues, race-to-the-bottom pricing, and inability to evaluate innovation or lifecycle costs
2. **The transparency paradox**: Georgia's system is among the world's most transparent, yet 45% of procurement still bypasses competitive processes through exemptions and simplified procurement. Transparency in the electronic system coexists with opacity in the exempted categories
3. **Barrier reduction as competition driver**: By making entry nearly free (GEL 50 fee, free documents, same-week registration), Georgia maximizes the bidder pool. This stands in stark contrast to the US, where high entry costs suppress competition
4. **Small market dynamics**: Georgia's entire annual procurement volume (~GEL 2.7 billion) is smaller than many single US federal contracts. The small market size means the competitive pool is inherently limited, making the low-barrier approach even more critical
5. **Digital-first as equalizer**: Remote participation from anywhere removes geographic barriers within Georgia (and for international bidders). This is directly relevant to the dissertation's examination of how procurement design choices affect competitive outcomes

### Comparison to US System

| Dimension | Georgia (Ge-GP) | United States (SAM.gov/Beta.SAM) |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| Registration cost | GEL 50 (~$19) participation fee | Free registration but $50K-$350K+ compliance |
| Registration time | 2-5 days | 2-8 weeks (SAM.gov alone) |
| Bid document cost | Free | Free (but CMMC/DCAA compliance adds $50K+) |
| Default evaluation | Lowest price (historically) | LPTA or best-value (depends on contract type) |
| Transparency | Real-time public observation | Limited (FPDS after-the-fact) |
| Protest timeline | 10 working days (DRB) | 100 days (GAO); years (Court of Federal Claims) |
| Annual procurement volume | ~$1 billion | ~$700 billion |
| Corruption perception | 3% (post-reform) | Low but persistent (cost-type contract abuse) |
