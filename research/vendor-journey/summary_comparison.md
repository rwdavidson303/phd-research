# Cross-Country Vendor Journey Comparison

## All 12 Countries vs. United States

**Purpose:** Side-by-side comparison of what it costs, how long it takes, and what barriers exist for a local vendor to compete for government contracts in each of the 13 systems studied. This data supports the dissertation's argument that procurement system design — not just evaluation method — determines who can afford to compete.

---

## Connection to Dissertation Research

### What the Cross-Country Data Reveals

1. **Barrier height determines competitive depth.** Countries with the lowest entry barriers (Chile, Georgia, Brazil) have the highest supplier participation rates relative to market size. When barriers are low, more firms compete — and the premise underlying both LPTA and best-value evaluation holds: that competition drives value.

2. **LPTA works differently in low-barrier vs. high-barrier systems.** Brazil's pregao delivers genuine 20% savings because hundreds of firms compete in real time. US LPTA often results in oligopoly pricing because only a handful of firms can afford to compete. The evaluation method's effectiveness is inseparable from the barrier structure.

3. **Best-value mandates correlate with simpler entry.** The UK (mandatory 10% social value), New Zealand (mandatory 10% economic benefits), and Australia (explicit "price is not the sole factor") all have qualification costs 5–50x lower than the US. This suggests that best-value evaluation may require lower barriers to function as intended — you need enough competitors for quality differentiation to matter.

4. **Digital infrastructure is the single most consistent predictor of low barriers.** Every system with qualification costs under $15,000 (Korea, Chile, Estonia, Georgia, Brazil) has full end-to-end digital procurement. The US remains the only system where digital is partial and fragmented.

5. **The US is the outlier, not the norm.** In every dimension measured — cost, time, complexity, digital maturity — the US federal procurement system imposes the highest barriers. This is not inherent to procurement complexity; it reflects specific policy choices that could be reformed.

### The Central Implication

> When qualification costs range from $425 (Chile) to $516,000+ (US), the evaluation method is not the primary determinant of who competes. The barrier structure is. This research argues that LPTA's dominance in US procurement compounds the barrier problem: in a system where only well-capitalized incumbents can afford to participate, choosing the lowest price simply rewards the firms with the deepest pockets — not the best value.
