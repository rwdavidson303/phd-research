# Qualitative Case Study Research Design: Understanding Source Selection Decision-Making in Federal Procurement

---

## 1. Introduction and Background

### 1.1 Research Problem

The United States federal government obligates over $700 billion annually in contracts with private-sector firms. The method by which the government selects contractors -- whether through Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA) evaluation or Best-Value Tradeoff evaluation -- is one of the most consequential design choices in the procurement process. Yet despite decades of policy debate, congressional mandates (NDAA Section 813, 2017), and practitioner advocacy, the empirical evidence on whether source selection method affects contract outcomes remains thin and inconclusive.

### 1.2 Quantitative Pilot Findings

A completed large-scale quantitative pilot study (N = 15,477 competitive federal service and IT contract awards, FY2024 Q2) tested whether Quality-Evaluating (QE) procurement design produces superior outcomes compared to Negotiated Fixed-Price (NFP) design. The results were striking:

- **No significant main effects** on cost growth (p = .377) or modification intensity (p = .449)
- **A marginal competition effect** (p = .085) suggesting QE may reduce single-bid outcomes by ~25%
- **One significant moderation finding** (p = .003): award size moderates the relationship, with QE design reducing cost growth for smaller procurements but not larger ones
- **Six of ten hypotheses were untestable** because they required data that archival sources do not contain

These findings establish that the source selection mechanism, by itself, does not predict aggregate contract cost performance. But they cannot explain why. The quantitative evidence points to a phenomenon that is context-dependent, mediated by organizational and human factors, and shaped by institutional dynamics that numbers alone cannot capture.

### 1.3 Purpose of This Study

This qualitative case study investigates the mechanisms, contextual factors, and practitioner reasoning that shape source selection decisions and their consequences. Where the quantitative pilot asked "does source selection method affect outcomes?" this study asks "how and why do contracting officers choose between LPTA and tradeoff, and how do the downstream dynamics of these contracts differ?"

## 2. Research Questions

### Overarching Research Question

**How and why do federal contracting officers choose between LPTA and best-value tradeoff source selection, and how do post-award contract dynamics differ as a result of that choice?**

### Specific Research Questions

Each specific question is derived from gaps identified in the quantitative pilot:

**RQ1: Decision Drivers.** What factors drive the choice between LPTA and tradeoff source selection in practice? What role do regulatory guidance, agency culture, workforce experience, leadership direction, and risk perception play in this decision?
- *Derived from untested H3 (solicitation quality as mediator) and H10 (standardized guidance effects)*

**RQ2: Administrative Burden vs. Evaluation Thoroughness.** How do contracting officers perceive and manage the trade-off between the administrative cost of tradeoff evaluation and the quality benefits it may provide? When do they judge the additional effort to be worthwhile?
- *Derived from untested H5 (oversight layer effects on schedule and cost)*

**RQ3: Post-Award Dynamics.** How do post-award contract management dynamics differ between LPTA-awarded and tradeoff-awarded contracts? Do contracting officers manage these contracts differently? Do contractor relationships evolve differently?
- *Derived from null findings on H1 (cost growth) and H2 (modifications) -- the quantitative evidence found no difference; do practitioners perceive one?*

**RQ4: Organizational and Workforce Factors.** What role do organizational culture, workforce competence, and leadership attention play in determining whether source selection method matters for outcomes?
- *Derived from the "fifth interpretation" in the pilot study's discussion: Kelman's (1990, 2005) argument that process quality matters more than process type*

**RQ5: The Complexity Mechanism.** Why does procurement complexity moderate the source selection method effect? What mechanisms explain the crossover interaction observed in the quantitative data?
- *Derived from the one supported hypothesis (H8, p = .003) -- the qualitative study seeks to explain the quantitative finding*

**RQ6: Market and Competition Dynamics.** How do market characteristics and competitive dynamics shape vendor participation decisions under different source selection methods? Do firms report that they are more likely to bid on tradeoff solicitations?
- *Derived from marginal H6 finding (competition effect, p = .085) and untested H7 (competition as mediator)*

## 3. Methodology Justification

### 3.1 Why Case Study?

The case study method is appropriate for this research for four reasons identified by Yin (2018):

1. **"How" and "why" questions.** The research questions ask how contracting officers make decisions, why they choose particular methods, and how post-award dynamics unfold -- questions that require explanatory, not descriptive, inquiry.

2. **Contemporary events.** Federal procurement source selection is an ongoing, evolving practice. The researcher cannot manipulate variables or conduct experiments; the goal is to understand a phenomenon as it occurs in its natural setting.

3. **Real-life context matters.** Source selection decisions are embedded in organizational, regulatory, political, and market contexts that cannot be separated from the phenomenon. Extracting the decision from its context (as a survey or experiment would) would strip away the very dynamics the study seeks to understand.

4. **Multiple sources of evidence.** Case studies draw on interviews, documents, archival records, and observation -- triangulating across data sources to build a comprehensive understanding.

### 3.2 Why Not Other Qualitative Approaches?

- **Grounded theory** (Glaser & Strauss, 1967; Charmaz, 2014): Not appropriate because existing theoretical frameworks (TCE, public value theory, incomplete contract theory) provide well-developed predictions. The goal is to refine and contextualize existing theory, not build theory from scratch.

- **Phenomenology** (Moustakas, 1994): Too focused on the lived experience of individual participants. The unit of analysis is the procurement decision within an organizational context, not the subjective experience of the decision-maker alone.

- **Ethnography** (Hammersley & Atkinson, 2019): Impractical for federal procurement settings, which involve classified or procurement-sensitive information, restricted access to evaluation boards, and dispersed activities across months. Extended participant observation is infeasible.

- **Narrative inquiry** (Clandinin & Connelly, 2000): Too individualistic. The research requires cross-case institutional analysis, not the construction of individual stories.

### 3.3 Epistemological Position

This study adopts a **post-positivist / critical realist** epistemology. There is an objective reality of procurement decision-making and contract performance, but that reality is accessed through participant interpretations, organizational narratives, and documentary evidence that require careful interpretation. The researcher brings prior knowledge (the quantitative pilot findings, professional experience in procurement) that informs but does not predetermine the analysis.

This position is consistent with the sequential explanatory mixed-methods design (Creswell & Creswell, 2018) that frames the broader research program.

## 4. Case Selection Strategy

### 4.1 Multiple-Case Embedded Design

The study employs a **multiple-case embedded design** (Yin, 2018, Type 4). Each "case" is a federal procurement program or agency acquisition function. Within each case, the embedded units of analysis are individual procurement actions and the decisions surrounding them.

### 4.2 Number of Cases

**Five cases** are recommended, following Eisenhardt's (1989) guidance that 4-10 cases provide a sufficient basis for analytical generalization while remaining feasible for a single researcher. Five cases balance depth (enough interviews and documents per case to achieve saturation) with breadth (enough cases to support cross-case pattern matching and replication logic).

### 4.3 Selection Logic: Maximum Variation Sampling

Cases will be selected using **maximum variation sampling** (Patton, 2015) to ensure that the findings reflect the full range of procurement contexts. Variation is sought across four dimensions:

**Dimension 1: Agency Type**
- 2-3 Department of Defense (DoD) agencies
- 2-3 civilian agencies
- Rationale: DoD and civilian agencies operate under different acquisition cultures, workforce structures, and oversight regimes. DoD is directly affected by NDAA Section 813 LPTA restrictions; civilian agencies are not.

**Dimension 2: Procurement Complexity**
- At least 2 cases involving smaller, less complex procurements ($250K - $5M)
- At least 2 cases involving larger, more complex procurements (>$5M)
- Rationale: The quantitative pilot found that complexity (proxied by award size) moderates the source selection effect. Case selection at different complexity levels enables investigation of this mechanism.

**Dimension 3: Source Selection Method Used**
- Cases where LPTA was used
- Cases where tradeoff was used
- Cases where the agency shifted between methods for comparable requirements
- Rationale: Comparing across methods enables cross-case analysis of decision factors and post-award dynamics.

**Dimension 4: Outcome Variation**
- At least one "successful" case (low cost growth, strong performance) per method
- At least one "problematic" case (high cost growth, modifications, disputes) per method
- Rationale: Positive and negative cases provide analytical contrast for understanding when and why source selection method matters.

### 4.4 Target Agencies (Illustrative)

| Case | Agency | Type | Complexity | Rationale |
|------|--------|------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) | DoD | Mixed | High-volume procurement; uses both LPTA and tradeoff; strong data in pilot sample |
| 2 | Air Force / Navy acquisition command | DoD | High | Directly affected by NDAA Sec. 813; complex technology procurements |
| 3 | GSA Federal Acquisition Service | Civilian | Mixed | Government-wide contract vehicles; procurement process leader |
| 4 | Department of Veterans Affairs | Civilian | High | Health IT procurement; high complexity; frequent cost growth issues |
| 5 | Department of Homeland Security | Civilian | Moderate | Border/cybersecurity tech; diverse procurement portfolio |

Final case selection will be confirmed based on access feasibility and the quantitative dataset's distribution of agencies.

### 4.5 Using the Quantitative Dataset for Case Identification

The 15,477-record quantitative dataset provides a systematic sampling frame for case identification. Specific procurements within target agencies can be identified based on:
- Source selection method used (QE vs. NFP)
- Award size and complexity indicators
- Cost growth and modification patterns
- Number of offers received

This quantitative-to-qualitative bridge ensures that case selection is empirically grounded rather than opportunistic.

## 5. Data Collection Methods

### 5.1 Semi-Structured Interviews

**Target Sample:** 25-35 interviews total across 5 cases (5-7 per case)

**Participant Categories:**

| Category | Role | Per Case | Total |
|----------|------|----------|-------|
| Contracting Officers (COs) | Led or managed the source selection | 2-3 | 10-15 |
| Contracting Officer Representatives (CORs) | Technical oversight post-award | 1-2 | 5-10 |
| Program Managers / Requirements Owners | Defined the requirement; evaluated outcomes | 1-2 | 5-10 |
| Industry Representatives | Contractors who bid on these programs | 0-1 | 3-5 |
| Legal Counsel / Procurement Analysts | Advised on evaluation strategy | 0-1 | 2-5 |

**Interview Parameters:**
- Duration: 45-60 minutes
- Format: Virtual (Zoom/Teams) with audio recording and automated transcription
- Approach: Semi-structured with an interview protocol (Section 5.2) that ensures consistency while allowing emergent themes
- Sampling within cases: Initial participants identified through case documentation; additional participants through snowball referral

### 5.2 Interview Protocol

#### Opening (5 minutes) -- Rapport and Context

1. Tell me about your role in the acquisition process. How long have you been doing this work?
2. Walk me through a recent procurement you managed from start to finish. What went well? What was challenging?

#### Source Selection Decision-Making (15-20 minutes) -- RQ1, RQ2

3. When you are planning a new procurement, how do you decide whether to use LPTA or best-value tradeoff evaluation?
4. What factors weigh most heavily in that decision? (Probe: regulatory guidance, agency policy, past experience, supervisor direction, protest risk, time pressure, workforce bandwidth)
5. How does your agency's culture or leadership influence the choice of source selection method?
6. Have you ever wanted to use one method but felt pressured to use another? What drove that pressure?
7. How do you think about the trade-off between the administrative burden of tradeoff evaluation and the potential quality benefits? When is the extra effort worth it?
8. How do bid protest risk and fear of protest influence your source selection decisions?

#### Post-Award Dynamics (15-20 minutes) -- RQ3, RQ4

9. In your experience, do contracts awarded through tradeoff evaluation play out differently after award compared to LPTA-awarded contracts? How so?
10. Can you describe a specific example where the source selection method seemed to affect the contractor relationship or contract performance?
11. How do you manage contracts that experience significant scope changes or cost growth? Does the original source selection method affect how you handle those situations?
12. What role does the quality of the original requirements document play in post-award success? Does source selection method affect requirements quality?
13. How important is the experience and skill of the contracting officer versus the formal evaluation method in determining contract outcomes?

#### Complexity and Context (10 minutes) -- RQ5

14. For complex, high-value procurements, does the source selection method matter more or less than for simpler, smaller ones? Why?
15. What makes a procurement "complex" from your perspective? (Probe: requirements uncertainty, technical risk, number of stakeholders, political visibility, performance period)
16. How do you adjust your procurement approach -- not just source selection, but the whole strategy -- for different levels of complexity?

#### Market and Competition (5-10 minutes) -- RQ6

17. How does the source selection method affect the number and quality of proposals you receive?
18. Have you observed contractors declining to bid on certain types of solicitations? What reasons do they give?
19. Do you think LPTA solicitations attract a different type of contractor than tradeoff solicitations?

#### Closing (5 minutes)

20. If you could change one thing about how source selection works in the federal government, what would it be?
21. Is there anything I should have asked about that I didn't?
22. Can you recommend other people I should speak with who have relevant experience?

### 5.3 Document Analysis

For each case, the following documents will be collected and analyzed:

| Document Type | Source | Purpose |
|---------------|--------|---------|
| Solicitation documents (RFP/RFQ) | SAM.gov, agency websites | Understand the evaluation criteria and requirements |
| Source selection plans (if releasable) | Agency FOIA or participant-provided | Understand the formal decision framework |
| Evaluation criteria and weighting | Solicitation documents | Compare formal criteria across methods |
| Post-award modification histories | USAspending.gov (existing data pipeline) | Triangulate interview accounts with objective data |
| GAO bid protest decisions (if applicable) | GAO.gov | Understand protest dynamics for specific cases |
| Agency acquisition strategies / policy memos | Agency websites, FOIA | Understand institutional context and guidance |
| Inspector General reports (if applicable) | Agency IG websites | Understand oversight findings |

### 5.4 Archival Data Integration

The quantitative dataset (15,477 awards) will be used as a supplementary data source within each case. For selected procurements, the archival record provides:
- Objective cost growth and modification data to triangulate with interview accounts
- Historical patterns of source selection method usage within the agency
- Comparison points for the specific procurements under study

This integration of quantitative archival data with qualitative interview data is a distinctive methodological contribution of the sequential design.

### 5.5 Observation (Opportunistic)

If access permits, the researcher will attend:
- Industry days or pre-solicitation conferences
- Source selection training events (DAU courses, NCMA workshops)
- Professional conference sessions on source selection topics

Field notes from these observations will provide supplementary contextual data. Observation is not a primary data source but an enrichment opportunity.

## 6. Analysis Approach

### 6.1 Analysis Framework

The analysis follows the systematic approach described by Miles, Huberman, and Saldana (2020), combining:
- **Data condensation:** Reducing interview transcripts and documents through coding
- **Data display:** Organizing coded data into matrices, networks, and visual displays
- **Conclusion drawing and verification:** Identifying patterns, building explanations, and testing conclusions against the data

### 6.2 Within-Case Analysis

For each of the five cases, the analysis will produce:

1. **Thick description** (Geertz, 1973): A detailed narrative of the procurement context, the source selection decision, the evaluation process, and post-award dynamics
2. **Chronological timeline:** Key decision points, critical incidents, and turning points in the procurement lifecycle
3. **Decision map:** A visual representation of the factors that influenced the source selection choice and their interrelationships
4. **Outcome assessment:** How the procurement performed, triangulated across interview accounts, documents, and archival data

### 6.3 Cross-Case Analysis

The cross-case analysis will employ three complementary techniques:

**Pattern Matching (Yin, 2018):** Compare observed patterns across cases to theoretical predictions derived from TCE, public value theory, and incomplete contract theory. For example:
- TCE predicts that complexity drives the choice of evaluation method. Do practitioners confirm this? What other factors does TCE miss?
- Public value theory predicts multidimensional outcome assessment. Do practitioners think about outcomes beyond price?
- The quantitative moderation finding predicts that source selection matters more for smaller procurements. Do practitioners' experiences confirm this?

**Explanation Building:** Iteratively construct causal explanations for the quantitative pilot findings. Why are the main effects null? What mechanisms produce the complexity moderation? What factors determine when source selection method matters and when it doesn't?

**Cross-Case Displays (Miles, Huberman & Saldana, 2020):**
- Case-ordered meta-matrix: All cases on one dimension, key themes on the other
- Predictor-outcome matrix: Source selection method x post-award outcomes x contextual factors
- Cross-case causal network: Visual model of the relationships between decision factors, method choice, and outcomes

### 6.4 Coding Strategy

Coding will follow a three-cycle approach (Saldana, 2021):

**First Cycle: Open/Initial Coding**
- Line-by-line coding of interview transcripts
- In vivo codes (participants' own language) and descriptive codes
- Goal: Generate a comprehensive initial code list without imposing theoretical structure

**Second Cycle: Focused/Axial Coding**
- Organize first-cycle codes into categories aligned with theoretical constructs
- Identify relationships between categories
- Begin connecting practitioner accounts to theoretical frameworks

**Third Cycle: Theoretical Coding**
- Map focused codes to the theoretical propositions
- Build cross-case propositions that explain the quantitative findings
- Identify emergent themes not captured by existing theory

### 6.5 Coding Architecture

| Code Group | Codes | Theoretical Link |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| **1. Decision Factors** | Regulatory guidance; Agency culture/norms; Protest risk perception; Administrative burden; Complexity assessment; Workforce experience; Leadership direction; Time pressure; Budget constraints | RQ1, RQ2 |
| **2. Post-Award Dynamics** | Contract management quality; Modification drivers; Contractor relationship; Scope evolution; Performance issues; Communication patterns; Trust; Adaptability | RQ3 |
| **3. Theoretical Constructs** | TCE: Asset specificity; TCE: Uncertainty; TCE: Frequency; Public Value: Substantive; Public Value: Legitimacy; Public Value: Operational feasibility; Incomplete contracts: Renegotiation; Scoring auction: Quality signaling | All RQs |
| **4. Organizational Factors** | Workforce competence; Training/development; Organizational learning; Agency risk tolerance; Process compliance vs. outcomes focus; Institutional memory | RQ4 |
| **5. Contextual Factors** | Agency type (civilian vs. DoD); Procurement size/complexity; Market conditions; Political environment; Oversight intensity; Technology maturity | RQ5 |
| **6. Market/Competition** | Firm participation decisions; Bid/no-bid factors; Proposal investment; Competitive landscape; Incumbent advantage; Small business dynamics | RQ6 |

## 7. Validity and Trustworthiness

Following Lincoln and Guba (1985) and Yin (2018), the study addresses four dimensions of research quality:

### 7.1 Credibility (Internal Validity)
- **Triangulation:** Multiple data sources per case (interviews + documents + archival data)
- **Member checking:** Share case narratives with key participants for accuracy verification
- **Prolonged engagement:** Multiple interview rounds if needed to clarify or deepen understanding
- **Peer debriefing:** Regular discussion with dissertation committee and qualitative methods advisor

### 7.2 Transferability (External Validity)
- **Thick description:** Provide enough contextual detail for readers to assess applicability to other procurement settings (state/local government, international, private sector)
- **Maximum variation case selection:** Increases the range of contexts represented
- **Analytical generalization** (Yin, 2018): Generalize to theory (refining TCE and public value theory as applied to procurement), not to population

### 7.3 Dependability (Reliability)
- **Audit trail:** Detailed research log maintained throughout the study
- **Codebook:** Formal codebook with definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and exemplar quotes
- **Inter-coder reliability:** A second coder will independently code 15-20% of transcripts; agreement will be assessed and discrepancies resolved through discussion

### 7.4 Confirmability (Objectivity)
- **Reflexivity journal:** Ongoing documentation of the researcher's professional background in procurement, assumptions, and potential biases
- **Negative case analysis:** Actively seek disconfirming evidence -- cases or accounts that contradict emerging themes
- **Transparent reporting:** All findings reported, including those that are unexpected or uncomfortable

## 9. IRB Considerations

### 9.1 Classification
- **Human subjects research:** Yes -- interviewing federal employees about their professional practices
- **Risk level:** Minimal risk (no sensitive personal information; professional practices only)
- **Expected review pathway:** Expedited review, Category 7 (research on individual or group characteristics or behavior)

### 9.2 Key Protections
- **Informed consent:** Written consent form with clear explanation of purpose, procedures, risks, benefits, and right to withdraw
- **De-identification:** Participants will be identified by role and agency type only (e.g., "a senior contracting officer at a civilian agency"), not by name or specific agency
- **Data security:** Audio recordings stored on encrypted, password-protected devices; transcripts de-identified before analysis; data retained for 5 years post-study then destroyed
- **No recording without explicit consent:** Participants may request that specific responses be off-the-record

### 9.3 Special Considerations for Federal Employee Participants

**Agency approval requirements:** Some federal agencies require employees to obtain supervisor or agency ethics office approval before participating in external research. The recruitment process must account for this:
- Contact agency Chief Procurement Officers (CPOs) or Heads of Contracting Activities (HCAs) first to secure institutional support
- Provide agencies with a research summary and interview protocol in advance
- Allow 4-8 weeks for agency approval processes

**Procurement-sensitive information:** The interview protocol is designed to elicit general practices and perceptions, NOT specific acquisition-sensitive information about ongoing procurements. Questions focus on:
- General decision-making patterns ("When you plan a procurement, how do you typically decide...")
- Retrospective accounts of completed procurements
- Professional judgments and perceptions about source selection effectiveness

**Hatch Act considerations:** Questions will not solicit political opinions or partisan views on procurement policy.

## 11. Expected Contributions

### Theoretical Contributions
- **Refine TCE** as applied to procurement: Specify the conditions under which governance mechanism choice (LPTA vs. tradeoff) matters for transaction outcomes, and when other factors dominate
- **Operationalize public value theory** in procurement: Move beyond normative prescription to empirically grounded understanding of how public value is (and is not) created through source selection
- **Advance incomplete contract theory:** Illuminate the mechanisms of post-award adaptation and how they interact with the initial selection method

### Methodological Contributions
- **Demonstrate the sequential explanatory design** with quantitative archival analysis informing qualitative case study: A model for future procurement research
- **Bridge the quantitative-qualitative divide** in public procurement scholarship: Show how null quantitative findings can motivate and guide productive qualitative inquiry

### Practical Contributions
- **Actionable guidance for contracting officers:** When is tradeoff evaluation worth the additional time and effort? Under what conditions does it matter?
- **Training and workforce development insights:** What skills and organizational supports do contracting officers need to make effective source selection decisions?
- **Acquisition reform guidance:** Evidence for policymakers considering further restrictions on LPTA or mandates for tradeoff evaluation

### Policy Contributions
- **Inform FAR revisions:** Provide evidence on when and how FAR guidance on source selection should be refined
- **Support congressional oversight:** Offer nuanced evidence on the effects of NDAA Section 813 restrictions
- **Advance evidence-based procurement policy:** Demonstrate that simple mandates (ban LPTA / require tradeoff) may be less effective than context-sensitive guidance

## 12. Limitations and Delimitations

### Delimitations (Intentional Boundaries)
- Focus on federal (not state/local) procurement
- Focus on service and IT procurements (not construction, commodities, or weapons systems)
- Focus on competitive procurements above the Simplified Acquisition Threshold ($250K)
- English-language interviews only

### Anticipated Limitations
- **Access constraints:** Federal agencies may limit or deny research participation
- **Retrospective accounts:** Participants may not accurately recall decision processes for past procurements
- **Social desirability bias:** Participants may present their decisions in a favorable light
- **Researcher bias:** The researcher's professional background in procurement creates both expertise and potential blind spots (addressed through reflexivity journal)
- **Generalizability:** Findings from 5 cases, while analytically generalizable to theory, cannot be statistically generalized to all federal procurements
