# Target Journal Strategy: Quantitative Source Selection Paper

## 1. Paper Summary and Positioning

**Working Title:** "From Lowest Price to Highest Public Value: A Quasi-Experimental Analysis of Source Selection Method Effects on Federal Contract Outcomes"

**Core Contribution:** The largest-sample quasi-experimental study of source selection method effects on federal contract outcomes to date (N = 15,477 awards; 3,869 in the primary comparison sample). The study provides the first systematic test using the FPDS source selection process field that became available in 2020.

**Key Selling Points:**
- Null main effects on cost growth and modification intensity challenge the prevailing policy assumption that tradeoff evaluation produces superior outcomes
- The significant complexity moderation finding (p = .003) -- source selection method matters for smaller procurements but not larger ones -- is novel and policy-relevant
- Rigorous methodology: propensity score matching, multiple model specifications, extensive robustness checks
- Transparent reporting of null results, contributing to the growing movement against publication bias in social science

**Paper Type:** Empirical, quantitative, cross-sectional archival analysis

## 2. Manuscript Preparation Notes

### Length Reduction Required
The current manuscript is approximately 78,500 words across 8 chapters -- far exceeding any journal's word limit. Most target journals require **8,000-12,000 words** (including references, tables, and figures). This requires cutting roughly 85-90% of the content.

### Recommended Approach
- **Keep:** Abstract, condensed introduction (2,000 words), focused lit review (2,500 words), methodology (2,500 words), results (2,500 words), discussion (2,000 words), references
- **Cut:** Extended theoretical background on all 10 hypotheses (focus on the 4 testable ones), detailed variable descriptions (move to online appendix), extensive robustness checks (summarize in text, detail in appendix)
- **Online Appendix:** Full variable operationalization table, PSM diagnostics, all robustness check results, NAICS/agency distribution tables
- **Reframe:** Lead with the null findings as the main contribution, not as a limitation

### Framing Strategy for Null Findings
The null results are the paper's strength, not its weakness. Frame around:
- The replication/credibility movement: well-powered null results advance knowledge (Franco et al., 2014)
- Policy relevance: billions of dollars in federal procurement decisions are made based on assumptions about source selection effects that this study does not confirm
- The moderation finding redirects the conversation: it's not "does method matter?" but "when does method matter?"

## 3. Tier 1: Premier Public Administration and Procurement Journals

### 3.1 Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory (JPART)
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Impact Factor:** ~7.2 (2024 JCR)
- **CiteScore:** ~9.8
- **Scope:** Empirical and theoretical research advancing public administration theory. Values rigorous quasi-experimental designs and theory-driven findings.
- **Fit:** **Strong.** JPART values methodological sophistication and theory-driven empirical work. The TCE and public value theory contributions align with the journal's core audience. The null-findings-as-informative framing would appeal to JPART's emphasis on advancing rather than simply confirming theory.
- **Recommended framing:** Lead with the theoretical contribution -- TCE and public value theory predict effects that the data do not confirm, suggesting the need for theoretical refinement. Emphasize the moderation finding as the empirical anchor for a more nuanced theory.
- **Review timeline:** 3-6 months
- **Acceptance rate:** ~10-15%
- **Recommendation:** **Submit here first.** Highest prestige, best theoretical fit.

### 3.2 Public Administration Review (PAR)
- **Publisher:** Wiley / American Society for Public Administration (ASPA)
- **Impact Factor:** ~4.9-6.2 (varies by database)
- **CiteScore:** ~12.5
- **Scope:** Broad public administration research; the flagship journal of the field. Highly competitive.
- **Fit:** **Good but competitive.** PAR publishes procurement research (Patrucco et al., 2023 was published here). The policy relevance is strong -- NDAA Section 813 restrictions, OFPP guidance on source selection. Risk: PAR may view the paper as too narrow (procurement-specific) for its generalist audience.
- **Recommended framing:** Lead with the policy puzzle -- Congress restricted LPTA but this study finds no evidence that tradeoff evaluation produces better cost outcomes. Frame as a cautionary tale about evidence-free policy.
- **Review timeline:** 4-8 months
- **Acceptance rate:** ~8-12%
- **Recommendation:** Strong alternative if JPART declines.

### 3.3 Journal of Public Procurement (JOPP)
- **Publisher:** Emerald
- **Impact Factor:** ~3.1 (2024)
- **CiteScore:** ~2.1
- **SJR:** Q2 (0.588)
- **Scope:** The field's flagship procurement journal. Publishes empirical, theoretical, and practitioner-oriented procurement research.
- **Fit:** **Excellent -- the natural home for this paper.** Directly on topic. The journal's audience (procurement scholars and practitioners) will immediately understand the significance of the findings. Prior key studies in this space (Rendon, 2009; Prier & McCue, 2009; Snider & Rendon, 2008) were published here.
- **Recommended framing:** Lead with the practical implications -- what do these findings mean for contracting officers making source selection decisions today? Emphasize the complexity moderation as actionable guidance.
- **Review timeline:** 2-4 months (typically faster than generalist journals)
- **Acceptance rate:** ~15-20%
- **Recommendation:** **Highest probability of acceptance.** Consider submitting here if prioritizing speed to publication over prestige.

## 4. Tier 2: Defense Acquisition and Government Management

### 4.1 Defense Acquisition Research Journal (DARJ)
- **Publisher:** Defense Acquisition University (DAU)
- **Scope:** Practitioner-oriented defense acquisition research. Bridges academic rigor and practitioner relevance.
- **Fit:** **Very good.** The DoD policy context (NDAA Section 813 LPTA restrictions) is directly relevant. DoD comprises ~30% of the study sample. DARJ values applied research with clear policy implications.
- **Recommended framing:** Emphasize the policy implications for DoD acquisition reform. Lead with the NDAA Section 813 context and what the findings mean for implementation.
- **Review timeline:** 2-3 months
- **Note:** Practitioner audience; may require less technical methodology and more policy discussion.
- **Recommendation:** Excellent outlet for a practitioner-focused version of the paper.

### 4.2 Journal of Defense Analytics and Logistics (JDAL)
- **Publisher:** Emerald
- **Scope:** Defense supply chain, logistics, and acquisition analytics.
- **Fit:** **Good.** The most directly comparable prior study (Osman, Gomez & Gunderson, 2017) was a Naval Postgraduate School thesis. JDAL bridges the defense acquisition and analytics communities.
- **Recommended framing:** Emphasize the analytical methodology (PSM, archival data analysis) as a template for defense acquisition research.
- **Review timeline:** 2-4 months

### 4.3 Public Performance and Management Review (PPMR)
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis
- **Impact Factor:** ~3.5
- **Scope:** Performance measurement and management in the public sector.
- **Fit:** **Moderate.** Frame around the performance measurement challenge -- cost growth as an imperfect proxy for public value, the CPARS data gap, and what procurement performance actually means.
- **Recommended framing:** Focus on the measurement contribution: what can and cannot be measured using archival procurement data?
- **Review timeline:** 3-5 months

## 5. Tier 3: Supply Chain and Procurement Management

### 5.1 Journal of Supply Chain Management (JSCM)
- **Publisher:** Wiley
- **Impact Factor:** ~8.6
- **Scope:** Supply chain management theory and practice. High prestige.
- **Fit:** **Moderate.** Would need to emphasize the TCE framework, buyer-supplier relationship implications, and the public procurement context as a testing ground for supply chain theory. The journal publishes relatively little public-sector work.
- **Recommended framing:** Position as a test of TCE in a large-sample public-sector context. The null findings challenge assumptions about evaluation mechanism effects that SCM literature often takes for granted.
- **Review timeline:** 3-6 months

### 5.2 Journal of Purchasing and Supply Management (JPSM)
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Impact Factor:** ~6.5
- **Scope:** Purchasing strategy, supplier management, procurement policy.
- **Fit:** **Moderate-Good.** Malacina et al. (2022) and other public procurement scholars have published here. The journal is receptive to public procurement research framed through supply management theory.
- **Recommended framing:** Supplier selection mechanisms and their post-selection performance effects. Position within the broader purchasing and supplier management literature.
- **Review timeline:** 3-5 months

### 5.3 International Journal of Public Sector Management (IJPSM)
- **Publisher:** Emerald
- **Impact Factor:** ~3.2
- **Scope:** Broad public sector management.
- **Fit:** **Good.** The journal welcomes procurement research as a subdomain of public sector management. Lower bar than JSCM or JPSM.

## 8. Key Preparation Steps

1. **Reduce manuscript to 10,000 words** -- the single biggest barrier to submission
2. **Write a compelling abstract** that leads with the null findings as a contribution, not a limitation
3. **Create an online appendix** with PSM diagnostics, robustness checks, variable details
4. **Update the literature review** to incorporate any 2025-2026 publications on source selection

6. **Register the study** on a preregistration platform (e.g., OSF) to signal commitment to transparency -- especially valuable for null results
