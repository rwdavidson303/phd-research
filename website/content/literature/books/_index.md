# Recommended Books for Dissertation Research

**Dissertation Title:** *From Lowest Price to Highest Public Value: Reimagining Government Procurement and RFP Evaluation*

**Last Updated:** February 2026

---

## How to Use This Guide

Each entry includes a full APA citation, a brief relevance description, and a priority classification:

- **Foundational** -- Essential reading; directly informs the theoretical framework or core arguments of the dissertation.
- **Important** -- Strongly recommended; provides significant supporting theory, methodology, or empirical context.
- **Supplementary** -- Useful for background, broader context, or specialized sub-topics.

---

## 1. Public Procurement and Government Contracting

### Textbooks and Practitioner Guides

Cibinic, J., Jr., Nash, R. C., Jr., & Yukins, C. R. (2011). *Formation of government contracts* (4th ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> The definitive treatise on how the U.S. federal government forms contracts, from planning through award. Essential for understanding the legal architecture of source selection, evaluation criteria, and the competitive negotiation process that this dissertation seeks to reform. **Foundational**

---

Cibinic, J., Jr., Nash, R. C., Jr., & Nagle, J. F. (2006). *Administration of government contracts* (4th ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> Comprehensive treatment of post-award contract administration, including modifications, disputes, and termination. Provides the institutional context for understanding how evaluation criteria at the formation stage affect downstream contract performance and public value delivery. **Important**

---

Nash, R. C., Jr., & Cibinic, J., Jr. (2011). *Competitive negotiation: The source selection process* (3rd ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> Focused specifically on the source selection process in competitive negotiations under FAR Part 15. Directly relevant to the dissertation's examination of how agencies weight price versus non-price factors and the mechanics of best-value tradeoff analysis. **Foundational**

---

Nash, R. C., Jr., Schooner, S. L., O'Brien-DeBakey, K. R., & Edwards, V. J. (2007). *The government contracts reference book: A comprehensive guide to the language of procurement* (3rd ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> Encyclopedic reference defining the terminology and concepts used throughout federal procurement. Invaluable for ensuring precise use of technical terms throughout the dissertation. **Supplementary**

---

Schooner, S. L. (2002). Desiderata: Objectives for a system of government contract law. *Public Procurement Law Review*, *11*(2), 103--118.

> While a journal article rather than a book, this widely cited work articulates the fundamental objectives that any procurement system should pursue -- competition, integrity, transparency, efficiency, customer satisfaction, and best value. Provides a normative framework that aligns with the dissertation's public value approach. **Foundational**

---

Thai, K. V. (Ed.). (2009). *International handbook of public procurement*. CRC Press/Taylor & Francis.

> The first comprehensive handbook covering public procurement from an international and interdisciplinary perspective. Includes chapters on procurement methods, e-procurement, ethics, and performance measurement across multiple countries. Essential for comparative context. **Important**

---

Arrowsmith, S. (2014). *The law of public and utilities procurement: Regulation in the EU and UK* (3rd ed., Vol. 1). Sweet & Maxwell.

> The leading treatise on EU and UK procurement law. Provides essential comparative perspective on how other legal regimes balance price and quality in public purchasing, and on the evolution of "most economically advantageous tender" (MEAT) criteria. **Important**

---

Arrowsmith, S., & Anderson, R. D. (Eds.). (2011). *The WTO regime on government procurement: Challenge and reform*. Cambridge University Press.

> Examines the international legal framework for government procurement under the WTO Government Procurement Agreement, including how signatory nations implement value-for-money principles. Relevant to understanding global norms around procurement evaluation. **Supplementary**

---

Arrowsmith, S., Linarelli, J., & Wallace, D. (2000). *Regulating public procurement: National and international perspectives*. Kluwer Law International.

> A comparative study of procurement regulation across jurisdictions, examining the tension between regulatory objectives such as value for money, competition, and social policy goals. Provides theoretical grounding for cross-national analysis. **Supplementary**

---

Dimitri, N., Piga, G., & Spagnolo, G. (Eds.). (2006). *Handbook of procurement*. Cambridge University Press.

> Brings together leading economists to analyze procurement through the lens of auction theory, contract theory, and industrial organization. Chapters cover scoring rules, multi-attribute auctions, and quality assessment -- all directly relevant to moving beyond lowest-price evaluation. **Foundational**

---

Albano, G. L., Ballarin, F., & Spagnolo, G. (2016). *The law and economics of framework agreements: Designing flexible solutions for public procurement*. Cambridge University Press.

> Combines legal and economic analysis of framework agreements, which are among the most important instruments in modern public procurement. Relevant to understanding how procurement design affects value outcomes over time. **Important**

---

National Association of State Procurement Officials. (2024). *State and local government procurement: A practical guide* (4th ed.). NASPO.

> The flagship practitioner reference for state and local procurement, covering the full procurement lifecycle. Provides essential context for understanding how sub-federal governments approach value-based procurement. **Supplementary**

---

Keyes, W. N. (2011). *Government contracts in a nutshell* (5th ed.). West Academic.

> A concise overview of federal government contract law covering the FAR system, competition requirements, contract types, pricing, and disputes. Useful as an accessible introduction and quick reference. **Supplementary**

---

Keyes, W. N. (2014). *Government contracts under the Federal Acquisition Regulation* (4th ed.). West Academic.

> A more comprehensive treatment than the Nutshell series, providing detailed coverage of the FAR and its implementation. Useful for understanding the regulatory framework governing evaluation criteria and award decisions. **Supplementary**

---

### Comparative and International Procurement

Racca, G. M., & Yukins, C. R. (Eds.). (2019). *Joint public procurement and innovation: Lessons across borders*. Bruylant.

> Examines how collaborative and joint procurement strategies across borders can drive innovation. Relevant to the dissertation's exploration of how procurement design can maximize public value beyond mere cost savings. **Important**

---

Racca, G. M., & Yukins, C. R. (Eds.). (2014). *Integrity and efficiency in sustainable public contracts: Balancing corruption concerns in public procurement internationally*. Bruylant.

> Explores the relationship between integrity, efficiency, and sustainability in public procurement across multiple jurisdictions. Directly relevant to the dissertation's argument that public value encompasses integrity and sustainability alongside cost. **Important**

---

Arrowsmith, S., & Quinot, G. (Eds.). (2013). *Public procurement regulation in Africa*. Cambridge University Press.

> Provides perspectives on procurement reform in developing contexts, where the tension between lowest price and broader value objectives is particularly acute. Useful for extending the dissertation's comparative analysis. **Supplementary**

## 2. Public Value Theory

Moore, M. H. (1995). *Creating public value: Strategic management in government*. Harvard University Press.

> The foundational text of public value theory. Moore argues that public managers should focus on creating "public value" from assets entrusted to them, using the "strategic triangle" of value, legitimacy, and operational capacity. This framework is the primary theoretical lens for the dissertation's argument that procurement should optimize for public value rather than lowest price. **Foundational**

---

Moore, M. H. (2013). *Recognizing public value*. Harvard University Press.

> Moore's follow-up to *Creating Public Value*, which refines and extends the theory with a focus on how to measure and recognize public value creation. Addresses the critical challenge of operationalizing public value -- directly relevant to designing procurement evaluation criteria that capture value dimensions beyond price. **Foundational**

---

Benington, J., & Moore, M. H. (Eds.). (2011). *Public value: Theory and practice*. Palgrave Macmillan.

> A collection of essays that assess, critique, and extend public value theory across different contexts and countries. Includes discussion of how public value relates to public services delivery, governance, and democratic accountability. Essential for grounding the dissertation's theoretical framework in the broader public value literature. **Foundational**

---

Bozeman, B. (2007). *Public values and public interest: Counterbalancing economic individualism*. Georgetown University Press.

> Bozeman's "public values" approach differs from Moore's public value framework by focusing on normative consensus about rights, benefits, and obligations. Provides an alternative theoretical lens and helps triangulate the dissertation's value framework. **Important**

---

Bryson, J. M., Crosby, B. C., & Bloomberg, L. (Eds.). (2015). *Creating public value in practice: Advancing the common good in a multi-sector, shared-power, no-one-wholly-in-charge world*. CRC Press.

> Applies public value creation concepts to practical governance challenges. Demonstrates how public value thinking can be operationalized in complex institutional settings like procurement. **Important**

---

Alford, J., & O'Flynn, J. (2009). *Rethinking public service delivery: Managing with external providers*. Palgrave Macmillan.

> Examines how governments manage relationships with external service providers, connecting public value theory to the practical realities of contracting and procurement. Bridges the gap between abstract value theory and procurement practice. **Important**

---

## 3. Transaction Cost Economics

Williamson, O. E. (1975). *Markets and hierarchies: Analysis and antitrust implications*. Free Press.

> The foundational work in transaction cost economics, establishing that the choice between markets and hierarchies depends on transaction characteristics (asset specificity, uncertainty, frequency). Provides the core theoretical framework for understanding why government procurement of complex goods and services cannot rely on price alone. **Foundational**

---

Williamson, O. E. (1985). *The economic institutions of capitalism: Firms, markets, relational contracting*. Free Press.

> Extends the TCE framework to encompass hybrid governance structures and relational contracting. Directly relevant to the dissertation's analysis of how procurement structures (contract types, evaluation criteria, relationship mechanisms) function as governance instruments that affect public value outcomes. **Foundational**

---

Williamson, O. E. (1996). *The mechanisms of governance*. Oxford University Press.

> Synthesizes Williamson's mature thinking on governance structures, transaction costs, and institutional design. Provides the analytical vocabulary for discussing how procurement evaluation criteria serve as governance mechanisms. **Important**

---

Coase, R. H. (1988). *The firm, the market, and the law*. University of Chicago Press.

> Collects Coase's seminal essays on transaction costs, property rights, and the nature of the firm. Provides the intellectual foundations upon which Williamson built TCE. Essential for understanding the origins of the theoretical framework. **Important**

---

## 4. Auction Theory and Mechanism Design

Milgrom, P. (2004). *Putting auction theory to work*. Cambridge University Press.

> Written by the 2020 Nobel laureate, this book bridges auction theory and practical market design. Covers multi-attribute auctions, scoring rules, and package auctions -- mechanisms directly analogous to procurement evaluation methods. Essential for understanding how mechanism design theory informs the shift from lowest-price to multi-criteria evaluation. **Foundational**

---

Klemperer, P. (2004). *Auctions: Theory and practice*. Princeton University Press.

> An accessible introduction to auction theory with emphasis on practical applications and notable real-world examples (including spectrum auctions). Provides theoretical grounding for understanding how procurement auctions function and why single-attribute (price-only) auctions can produce suboptimal outcomes. **Important**

---

Krishna, V. (2009). *Auction theory* (2nd ed.). Academic Press.

> The standard graduate textbook on auction theory, providing rigorous formal treatment of single-object and multi-object auctions, revenue equivalence, optimal auctions, and collusion. Provides the mathematical foundations for analyzing procurement mechanisms. **Important**

---

Milgrom, P. (2017). *Discovering prices: Auction design in markets with complex constraints*. Columbia University Press.

> Describes the development and implementation of the incentive auction for radio spectrum, demonstrating how sophisticated mechanism design can maximize value in complex settings. Provides inspiration for analogous innovations in procurement design. **Supplementary**

---

Cramton, P., Shoham, Y., & Steinberg, R. (Eds.). (2006). *Combinatorial auctions*. MIT Press.

> Comprehensive treatment of combinatorial auctions where bidders can bid on packages of items. Relevant to understanding multi-lot and bundled procurement strategies that can enhance value outcomes. **Supplementary**

---

## 5. Contract Theory and Incentive Design

Laffont, J.-J., & Tirole, J. (1993). *A theory of incentives in procurement and regulation*. MIT Press.

> The definitive treatment of principal-agent theory applied to procurement and regulation. Develops the formal models for understanding how information asymmetry between government buyers and private sellers affects contract design and evaluation. The theoretical backbone for the dissertation's analysis of why price-only evaluation fails under asymmetric information. **Foundational**

---

Bolton, P., & Dewatripont, M. (2005). *Contract theory*. MIT Press.

> The comprehensive graduate textbook on contract theory, covering moral hazard, adverse selection, and incomplete contracts. Provides the theoretical toolkit for analyzing how procurement evaluation criteria create incentive structures that affect contractor behavior and public value outcomes. **Foundational**

---

Tirole, J. (1988). *The theory of industrial organization*. MIT Press.

> The standard graduate textbook on industrial organization theory, including market structure, strategic behavior, and regulation. Provides the microeconomic foundations for understanding supplier markets and competitive dynamics in procurement. **Important**

---

Hart, O. (1995). *Firms, contracts, and financial structure*. Oxford University Press.

> Develops the incomplete contracts framework, arguing that when contracts cannot specify all contingencies, the allocation of residual control rights matters. Relevant to understanding why procurement evaluation must consider factors beyond price -- because contracts are inherently incomplete. **Important**

---

Salanié, B. (2005). *The economics of contracts: A primer* (2nd ed.). MIT Press.

> An accessible introduction to contract theory covering moral hazard, adverse selection, and incentive design. Useful as an entry point to the formal economics of procurement incentive structures. **Supplementary**

---

## 6. Public Administration Research Methods

Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2022). *Research design: Qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods approaches* (6th ed.). SAGE.

> The standard reference for research design in the social sciences, including public administration. Provides the overarching methodological framework for the dissertation's multi-method approach to studying procurement outcomes. **Foundational**

---

Creswell, J. W., & Plano Clark, V. L. (2018). *Designing and conducting mixed methods research* (3rd ed.). SAGE.

> The definitive guide to mixed methods research design, covering convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential, and embedded designs. Essential for structuring the dissertation's combination of quantitative procurement outcome analysis with qualitative case studies of value-based evaluation. **Foundational**

---

Shadish, W. R., Cook, T. D., & Campbell, D. T. (2002). *Experimental and quasi-experimental designs for generalized causal inference*. Houghton Mifflin.

> The gold standard reference for quasi-experimental research design, covering validity threats, regression discontinuity, interrupted time series, and difference-in-differences. Essential for the dissertation's empirical strategy of measuring the causal effects of evaluation criteria changes on procurement outcomes. **Foundational**

---

Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J.-S. (2009). *Mostly harmless econometrics: An empiricist's companion*. Princeton University Press.

> A practical guide to applied econometrics emphasizing causal inference through instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences. Directly relevant to the dissertation's quantitative analysis of procurement data. **Important**

---

Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J.-S. (2015). *Mastering 'metrics: The path from cause to effect*. Princeton University Press.

> A more accessible introduction to the five core econometric tools (randomized trials, regression, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences). Useful as an accessible complement to *Mostly Harmless Econometrics*. **Supplementary**

---

Yin, R. K. (2018). *Case study research and applications: Design and methods* (6th ed.). SAGE.

> The standard text on case study methodology, covering single-case and multiple-case designs, data collection, and analysis. Essential for the dissertation's case study components examining specific agencies' transitions from lowest-price to best-value procurement. **Important**

---

King, G., Keohane, R. O., & Verba, S. (1994). *Designing social inquiry: Scientific inference in qualitative research*. Princeton University Press.

> A landmark text on applying the logic of scientific inference to qualitative research. Relevant to ensuring the dissertation's qualitative components meet rigorous standards of causal reasoning. **Supplementary**

---

Patton, M. Q. (2015). *Qualitative research and evaluation methods* (4th ed.). SAGE.

> Comprehensive treatment of qualitative methods including interviewing, observation, and document analysis. Useful for the dissertation's qualitative data collection from procurement professionals and stakeholders. **Important**

---

Gerring, J. (2017). *Case study research: Principles and practices* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.

> Provides a rigorous methodological framework for case study selection, design, and analysis. Complements Yin's text with a more formalized approach to case selection logic. **Supplementary**

---

## 7. Federal Acquisition System

Cibinic, J., Jr., Nash, R. C., Jr., & Nagle, J. F. (2004). *Cost-reimbursement contracting* (3rd ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> Comprehensive treatment of cost-reimbursement contracts, which predominate in complex acquisitions where price certainty is low. Directly relevant to understanding why cost-type contracts require evaluation criteria that go well beyond price. **Important**

---

Edwards, V. J. (2006). *The source selection answer book* (2nd ed.). Management Concepts.

> A practical guide to source selection procedures, including evaluation criteria design, proposal evaluation, and award decisions. Bridges the gap between legal/regulatory requirements and actual procurement practice. **Important**

---

Feldman, S. W. (2016). *Government contract guidebook* (5th ed.). Thomson Reuters.

> A comprehensive one-volume guide to federal government contracting covering the entire procurement lifecycle. Useful as a reference for understanding the regulatory and legal environment within which evaluation criteria operate. **Supplementary**

---

Manuel, K. M. (2015). *Evaluating the "past performance" of federal government contractors: Legal requirements and issues*. Congressional Research Service (CRS Report R41562).

> A detailed analysis of the legal framework for evaluating past performance in federal procurement. Directly relevant to the dissertation's examination of non-price evaluation factors and their role in driving public value. **Important**

---

Schwartz, M. (2014). *Defense acquisitions: How DOD acquires weapon systems and recent efforts to reform the process*. Congressional Research Service (CRS Report RL34026).

> Comprehensive overview of the defense acquisition process, including source selection procedures. Provides essential context for understanding how the largest procurement organization in the world balances cost and non-cost factors. **Important**

---

Government Accountability Office. (2024). *Principles of federal appropriations law* (4th ed.). GAO.

> The "Red Book" -- the authoritative reference on federal fiscal law governing how agencies may spend appropriated funds. Provides the legal backdrop for procurement evaluation decisions and the constraints on pursuing public value. **Supplementary**

---

Nash, R. C., Jr., & Rawicz, L. (2008). *Intellectual property in government contracts* (6th ed.). Wolters Kluwer.

> Covers the complex rules governing intellectual property rights in government contracts. Relevant to understanding how IP considerations -- a non-price factor -- affect procurement value and should be reflected in evaluation criteria. **Supplementary**

---

## 8. Government Contract and Procurement Law

Schooner, S. L., & Yukins, C. R. (2019). Government contracts law: Cases and materials. In *GW Law Faculty Publications*. George Washington University Law School.

> Casebook used in the leading government procurement law program, covering the full range of legal issues in federal contracting. Provides the legal framework within which procurement evaluation operates. **Important**

---

Manos, K. L. (2012). *Government contract costs and pricing* (2nd ed.). Thomson Reuters.

> The definitive text on government contract cost and pricing, covering cost accounting standards, cost allowability, and defective pricing. Essential for understanding the technical dimensions of price evaluation in procurement. **Important**

---

Cibinic, J., Jr., & Nash, R. C., Jr. (Eds.). (1987--present). *The Nash & Cibinic Report*. Wolters Kluwer (monthly publication).

> The leading monthly analytical publication on government contract law and policy. An indispensable ongoing resource for tracking developments in procurement evaluation doctrine and practice. **Foundational**

---

Schaengold, M., Guiffré, T., & Gill, R. (2019). *Government contract law: The deskbook for procurement professionals* (4th ed.). American Bar Association.

> A practitioner-oriented overview of government contract law produced by the ABA Section of Public Contract Law. Useful reference for understanding legal constraints and opportunities in procurement evaluation design. **Supplementary**

---

## 9. Procurement Reform, Innovation, and Modernization

Kelman, S. (1990). *Procurement and public management: The fear of discretion and the quality of government performance*. AEI Press.

> Kelman's groundbreaking argument that excessive rule-based procurement constrains managerial discretion and undermines performance. A direct intellectual precursor to the dissertation's argument for value-based over price-based evaluation. **Foundational**

---

Kelman, S. (2005). *Unleashing change: A study of organizational change in government*. Brookings Institution Press.

> Examines the Federal Acquisition Streamlining Act (FASA) and Federal Acquisition Reform Act (FARA) reforms of the 1990s as a case study in government organizational change. Provides historical context for understanding prior attempts to shift procurement from price-focused to value-focused evaluation. **Important**

---

Gomes, P. C. (2021). *EU public procurement and innovation: The innovation partnership procedure and harmonization challenges*. Edward Elgar.

> Analyzes the European Union's Innovation Partnership Procedure, a procurement method specifically designed to support innovation. Provides international comparative perspective on how procurement design can optimize for value beyond lowest price. **Important**

---

Edler, J., & Georghiou, L. (Eds.). (2015). *Public procurement for innovation*. Edward Elgar.

> Examines public procurement as a demand-side innovation policy tool, with case studies from multiple countries. Relevant to the dissertation's argument that value-based procurement can drive innovation and broader social outcomes. **Important**

---

Goldsmith, S., & Eggers, W. D. (2004). *Governing by network: The new shape of the public sector*. Brookings Institution Press.

> Examines how government increasingly delivers services through networks of public and private providers, requiring procurement approaches that optimize relationships and outcomes rather than just purchase price. **Supplementary**

---

Kettl, D. F. (2016). *Sharing power: Public governance and private markets*. Brookings Institution Press.

> Analyzes the challenges of governing through contracts and markets, including the limits of competitive procurement and the need for governance mechanisms that ensure public value. **Supplementary**

---

Light, P. C. (2008). *A government ill executed: The decline of the federal service and how to reverse it*. Harvard University Press.

> Documents the decline of federal capacity, including the acquisition workforce, and its implications for government performance. Provides context for understanding why procurement reform requires attention to institutional capacity. **Supplementary**

---

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development. (2024). *Public procurement for public sector innovation*. OECD Publishing.

> An OECD policy framework examining how public procurement can be leveraged as a strategic tool for innovation. Provides international best practices and benchmarks relevant to the dissertation's reform proposals. **Important**

---

## 10. Behavioral Economics and Decision-Making in Procurement

Thaler, R. H., & Sunstein, C. R. (2008). *Nudge: Improving decisions about health, wealth, and happiness*. Yale University Press.

> Introduces the concept of "choice architecture" -- how the design of decision environments affects outcomes. Relevant to understanding how the structure of procurement evaluation criteria shapes both bidder behavior and evaluator decision-making. **Supplementary**

---

Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, fast and slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.

> Summarizes decades of research on cognitive biases and heuristics in human judgment. Relevant to understanding how procurement evaluators process multi-attribute information and why structured evaluation criteria are essential for consistent, value-maximizing decisions. **Supplementary**

---

Bazerman, M. H., & Moore, D. A. (2013). *Judgment in managerial decision making* (8th ed.). Wiley.

> Examines cognitive biases in managerial contexts, including anchoring, overconfidence, and the winner's curse. Directly applicable to understanding decision biases in source selection and evaluation. **Supplementary**

---

## 11. New Public Management and Governance Theory

Hood, C. (1991). A public management for all seasons? *Public Administration*, *69*(1), 3--19.

> The landmark article defining New Public Management (NPM), which emphasized market mechanisms and competition in government. While an article, it is included here because it is foundational for understanding the intellectual origins of lowest-price procurement ideology. **Foundational**

---

Osborne, S. P. (Ed.). (2010). *The new public governance? Emerging perspectives on the theory and practice of public governance*. Routledge.

> A collection that moves beyond NPM to articulate "New Public Governance" -- a relational, network-based model of public service delivery. Provides the governance theory context for understanding why value-based procurement requires different institutional arrangements than price-based purchasing. **Important**

---

Denhardt, J. V., & Denhardt, R. B. (2015). *The new public service: Serving, not steering* (4th ed.). Routledge.

> Challenges the market-oriented assumptions of NPM by emphasizing democratic values, citizenship, and the public interest. Provides normative grounding for the dissertation's argument that procurement should serve public value, not just economic efficiency. **Supplementary**

---

Pollitt, C., & Bouckaert, G. (2017). *Public management reform: A comparative analysis into the age of austerity* (4th ed.). Oxford University Press.

> A comparative analysis of public management reform across OECD countries, including procurement reform. Provides international context for understanding how different reform trajectories have affected procurement evaluation practices. **Supplementary**
