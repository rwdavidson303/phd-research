> **US barriers are 87x higher** than the lowest-barrier procurement systems. This section maps the vendor journey in 12 countries and reveals how system design — not evaluation method — determines who can afford to compete.

## US Market Entry Highlights

Several international markets are **cheaper to enter from the US** than the US domestic market itself:

| Market | US Vendor First-Year Entry Cost | Domestic Comparison |
|--------|-------------------------------:|---------------------|
| Estonia (e-Residency path) | $8,700 – $45,800 | Lower than US domestic floor |
| Chile (US-Chile FTA) | $12,000 – $65,000 | Lower than US domestic median |
| Georgia | $14,800 – $74,000 | Lower than US domestic median |
| **US Domestic** | **$30,000 – $1,100,000+** | **Baseline** |
| EU (multi-state) | $218,000 – $1,635,000+ | Higher than US domestic |

**The US federal procurement system's barriers are not just high by international standards — they may be the highest in the world.**

## Key Findings

1. **Barrier height determines competitive depth.** Countries with the lowest entry barriers have the highest supplier participation rates relative to market size.

2. **LPTA works differently in low-barrier vs. high-barrier systems.** Brazil's pregao delivers genuine 20% savings because hundreds of firms compete in real time. US LPTA often results in oligopoly pricing because only a handful of firms can afford to compete.

3. **Best-value mandates correlate with simpler entry.** The UK, New Zealand, and Australia all have qualification costs 5–50x lower than the US — suggesting best-value evaluation needs enough competitors for quality differentiation to matter.

4. **Digital infrastructure predicts low barriers.** Every system with qualification costs under $15,000 has full end-to-end digital procurement. The US remains the only system where digital is partial and fragmented.

5. **The US is the outlier, not the norm.** In every dimension measured — cost, time, complexity, digital maturity — the US imposes the highest barriers of any system studied.
