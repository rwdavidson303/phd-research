<h2 class="vj-section-title">The Qualification Gauntlet</h2>
<p class="vj-section-subtitle">28 steps before you earn $1 — 15 just to be <em>eligible</em> to bid on a federal contract</p>

<div class="vj-timeline">

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">1</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Form Legal Business Entity</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">$100–$800</span>
      </div>
      <p>LLC, S-Corp, or C-Corp — register with your state, draft operating agreements, set up governance structure. The legal foundation for everything else.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">2</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Get EIN from IRS</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>Employer Identification Number — the federal tax ID every business needs. Quick online application, but required before bank accounts or SAM registration.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">3</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Open Business Bank Account</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free–$25/mo</span>
      </div>
      <p>Government payments require a dedicated business bank account. Needed for ACH direct deposits and to separate personal from business finances.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">4</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Identify NAICS Codes</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>Choose from 6,000+ North American Industry Classification System codes. These determine which contracts you can bid on, your size standard, and set-aside eligibility.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">5</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Obtain UEI Number</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>Unique Entity Identifier — replaced DUNS numbers in 2022. Assigned through SAM.gov, this is your company's universal ID across all federal systems.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">6</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Register on SAM.gov</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free (30–60 min)</span>
      </div>
      <p>System for Award Management — the mandatory gateway for all federal contractors. Initial registration takes hours; must be renewed <strong>annually</strong> or you lose eligibility mid-contract.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">7</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Determine SBA Size Standard</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>The Small Business Administration sets revenue and employee thresholds for each NAICS code. Your size status controls access to billions in small business set-asides.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">8</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>DCAA-Compliant Accounting System</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">$4,200–$200,000+/yr</span>
      </div>
      <p>Defense Contract Audit Agency requires cost-accounting systems that track direct/indirect costs, labor rates, and overhead pools. Most commercial accounting software doesn't qualify — specialized systems or consultants are required.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">9</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Socioeconomic Certifications</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free (heavy documentation)</span>
      </div>
      <p>8(a) Business Development, HUBZone, Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB), Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned (SDVOSB). Each certification opens set-aside opportunities but requires months of documentation and ongoing compliance.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">10</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>CMMC Certification (DoD)</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">$5,000–$300,000</span>
      </div>
      <p>Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification — required for Department of Defense contracts. Level 2+ demands third-party assessment. The cost of IT infrastructure upgrades alone can exceed $100K for small firms.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">11</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Surety Bonding (Construction)</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">1–5% of contract value</span>
      </div>
      <p>Bid bonds, performance bonds, and payment bonds — required for construction contracts over $150K. Bond capacity depends on your financial history, locking out younger firms.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">12</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Required Insurance</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">$2,000–$10,000+/yr</span>
      </div>
      <p>General liability, professional liability (E&O), workers' comp, cyber liability. Specific coverage requirements vary by contract type and agency.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">13</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Capability Statement</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">$500–$5,000</span>
      </div>
      <p>A 1–2 page marketing document — essentially your resume for government buyers. Core competencies, past performance, differentiators, NAICS codes, and socioeconomic status. Professional design matters.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">14</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Build Past Performance</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">Opportunity cost: years</span>
      </div>
      <p>The ultimate Catch-22 of federal contracting: you need past performance to win contracts, but you need contracts to build past performance. Most new entrants start as subcontractors, accepting low margins for years to build a record.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">15</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Claim SBS Profile</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>Small Business Search profile on SAM.gov — where contracting officers and prime contractors find small businesses. An incomplete profile means missed teaming and subcontracting opportunities.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

</div><!-- /vj-timeline -->

<h2 class="vj-section-title">The Bidding Maze</h2>
<p class="vj-section-subtitle">13 steps to respond to a <em>single</em> RFP — with no guarantee of winning</p>

<div class="vj-timeline">

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">1</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Monitor SAM.gov Daily</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free (time-intensive)</span>
      </div>
      <p>Thousands of opportunities posted weekly. Finding the right ones requires daily monitoring, complex keyword searches, and understanding agency-specific posting patterns.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">2</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Review the RFP</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>A typical solicitation runs 50–200+ pages of requirements, evaluation criteria, special clauses, and compliance mandates. Reading it carefully is a multi-day effort — and most won't be worth pursuing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">3</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Attend Industry Day</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">$500–$5,000</span>
      </div>
      <p>Pre-solicitation conferences where agencies brief potential bidders. Often in-person (travel costs), and sometimes the only way to get real insight into what the agency actually wants.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">4</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Submit Questions During Q&A</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p>Formal question-and-answer period where vendors seek clarification. Answers are typically published to all bidders. Strategic questioning is an art — revealing too much tips off competitors.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">5</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Prepare Technical Proposal</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">$15,000–$150,000+</span>
      </div>
      <p>The core of your bid: technical approach, management plan, staffing, quality assurance, transition plan. Large proposals require dedicated teams of writers, SMEs, and reviewers working for weeks.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">6</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Prepare Cost/Price Proposal</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">$5,000–$50,000</span>
      </div>
      <p>Detailed cost volumes with labor categories, rates, indirect rates, fee structures, and basis-of-estimate. Must be consistent with the technical approach and defensible under audit.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">7</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Assemble Past Performance Volume</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--cost">$2,000–$10,000</span>
      </div>
      <p>Documented evidence of relevant contract performance — project descriptions, client references, CPARS ratings. References must be contactable and willing to vouch for you on this specific type of work.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">8</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Submit by Deadline</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--free">Free</span>
      </div>
      <p><strong>Late = disqualified. No exceptions.</strong> Even a 1-minute delay after the deadline means your entire investment is wasted. Electronic submission systems sometimes crash on deadline day.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">9</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Government Evaluation</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--wait">Weeks to months</span>
      </div>
      <p>Evaluation boards score proposals against stated criteria. For best-value procurements, technical merit may outweigh price. For LPTA, only the cheapest technically acceptable proposal wins.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">10</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Discussions & Negotiations</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--wait">Additional weeks</span>
      </div>
      <p>In best-value procurements, the government may open discussions with firms in the competitive range. LPTA procurements typically skip this — another reason best-value is more nuanced but also more equitable.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">11</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Award Decision</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--wait">—</span>
      </div>
      <p>The contracting officer selects a winner. Losers receive a brief debriefing explaining the rationale — often vague enough to be unhelpful for improving future bids.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">12</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Potential Bid Protest</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--high">$50,000–$250,000 legal</span>
      </div>
      <p>If you believe the evaluation was unfair, you can protest to GAO or the Court of Federal Claims. Legal fees are enormous, outcomes uncertain, and the process can delay the entire program by 100+ days.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="vj-step">
    <div class="vj-step-marker">13</div>
    <div class="vj-step-content">
      <div class="vj-step-header">
        <h3>Post-Award Conference & Kickoff</h3>
        <span class="vj-badge vj-badge--wait">1–4 weeks</span>
      </div>
      <p>Transition planning, security clearance processing, badging, IT access. Even after winning, it can take weeks before you start earning revenue on the contract.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

</div><!-- /vj-timeline -->

<h2 class="vj-section-title">Why This Matters for Public Value</h2>

When the barrier to entry is this high, the competitive pool shrinks. Fewer bidders means less innovation, less price pressure, and more incumbency advantage. The vendors who can afford to play the game aren't necessarily the ones who deliver the best outcomes — they're the ones with the deepest pockets and the most patience for bureaucracy.

**This is the core tension this dissertation investigates:**

> When the process is this expensive and complex, does choosing the lowest-price bidder actually maximize public value? Or does it simply reward the firms most willing to absorb sunk costs — while shutting out potentially superior competitors who can't afford to play?

The evidence suggests that **best-value tradeoff procurement** — which weighs technical merit, past performance, and innovation alongside price — produces better outcomes precisely *because* it gives the evaluation process a way to see past the price tag. LPTA, by contrast, compresses all that investment into a single question: *who bid lowest?*

In a system where it costs $30K–$1.1M+ just to compete, the answer to that question may not serve the public interest.
